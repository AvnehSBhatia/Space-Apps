# Submission checklist

Taken from the official [Project Submission Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/)
and [Participant FAQs](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/participant-faqs/), read on 15 November 2026.
If anything here disagrees with those pages, the pages win.

## Deadline

**Sunday 15 November 2026, 11:59 p.m.** in the time zone of our Local Event. If we are in the Universal Event,
it is 11:59 p.m. where the Team Owner lives. Anything saved or submitted after that is not judged.

We can submit early and resubmit as often as we like; only the last saved submission is judged. Submit a rough
version first, then improve it.

## Before we can submit

- [ ] Every team member has a Space Apps account and has registered for the 2026 hackathon
- [ ] Every team member is a confirmed participant at the **same** Local Event (or all in the Universal Event)
- [ ] The team exists on the site, with at most six members (a Universal Event team needs at least two)
- [ ] The team has selected the challenge: Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer
- [ ] Anyone under 18 was registered by a parent or legal guardian

The Project tab is at profile icon → My Team → Project → Edit. Use "Save Progress" often, and turn the edit lock on
while editing so teammates do not overwrite each other.

## The project form

Everything must be in English. Every required field must be filled or the Submit button will not work.

- [ ] **Project Name**
- [ ] **High-Level Project Summary**: what we built, how it addresses the challenge, why it matters
- [ ] **Link to Project Demo**: a video of 30 seconds or less, or a slide deck of at most 7 slides including the
      title and intro slides. Going over either limit makes the project ineligible for Global Judging.
- [ ] **Link to Final Project**: the public repository or the deployed site
- [ ] **Detailed Project Description**: what it does, how it works, benefits, goals, tools and languages used
- [ ] **Use of Super Intelligence (SI)**: say which AI tools we used and how (copy from [AI_USE.md](AI_USE.md))
- [ ] **NASA Data**: exactly which NASA data and resources we used. At least one is required for a Global Award.
- [ ] **Space Agency Partner Data and Other References**: every dataset, library, tool, piece of code, text and
      image we used, including open-source ones and any of our own earlier code

Then tick "Terms and Conditions", tick "Confirm All Work is Original", and press **Submit for Judging**. A banner
confirms it went through.

## Links

- [ ] Every link opens with no login, password, permission request or sign-up
- [ ] Every link was tested in a private browser window
- [ ] Videos and documents are hosted elsewhere (YouTube, Google Drive, GitHub); the form only accepts images
      (JPG, PNG, GIF up to 10 MB)
- [ ] Optional: if the project is on a new website, request a FortiGuard web filter classification so judges'
      networks do not block it (the form is linked from the Project Submission Guide; company name "N/A")

## Rules that are easy to break

- **AI use must be marked.** Images and video made with AI need a visible watermark. AI-made audio, code and
  data must be acknowledged in descriptive text and in metadata. The guide also says AI should not be used to
  come up with the idea for how to solve the challenge.
- **No NASA branding** in anything generated: no NASA logos, flags or mission identifiers.
- **Nobody under 18 in the demo or the project.** No photos, video, voices or likenesses of anyone under 18,
  including ourselves. A slide deck or a screen recording with captions avoids the problem.
- **A slide deck must show** the team name, the names of the team members and the challenge we selected.
- **One team, one challenge.** Work on the challenge may not start before the hackathon.

## How projects are judged

Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance and Presentation. The demo counts toward Presentation; most of the
score comes from the project page itself.
