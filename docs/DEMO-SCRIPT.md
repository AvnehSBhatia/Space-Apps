# Demo script

The demo is a video of **30 seconds or less**, or a slide deck of **at most 7 slides**. Going over either limit
makes the project ineligible for Global Judging. Aim for 28 seconds.

Rules that apply to it:

- English. Captions on screen carry the story, so it needs no voice.
- No faces, voices or likenesses of anyone under 18. Use a screen recording with captions.
- If any picture, video or voice in it was made with an AI tool, it needs a visible watermark (images, video)
  or an acknowledgement in the description and metadata (audio).
- Host it where anyone can open it without logging in (YouTube set to Unlisted or Public, or a Google Drive
  link set to "Anyone with the link"). Test the link in a private window.

## 30-second video

Record the dust storm mission in one take at 1080p with the browser zoomed to 125%. Captions in large type at
the bottom.

| Time | On screen | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:02 | Title card: team name, "2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge" | Team <NAME>. Challenge: Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer. |
| 0:02 to 0:05 | The outpost and its four crew | Most space games for kids use made-up numbers. |
| 0:05 to 0:09 | Game title and tagline | SolCast. Keep a Mars crew alive against the real Mars record. |
| 0:09 to 0:14 | Morning report: sunlight drops to Low, the sky turns brown | Every day is real data from NASA's Curiosity rover. This is the 2018 global dust storm. |
| 0:14 to 0:19 | Drag the slider, press Huddle, press Lock it in | Spend energy on air and food, or save it for the night? |
| 0:19 to 0:23 | The reveal: "Real data. Curiosity rover, sol 2085." The crew reacts. Tap a number to show its NASA source. | Then the real measurement decides. Every number shows its source. |
| 0:23 to 0:27 | Debrief card | "When the sky went dark you had two spare nights of battery." In this storm, the Opportunity rover never woke up. |
| 0:27 to 0:30 | Home screen with the link | Free in any browser. Next: classroom tournaments and solar storm shelters. |

Before recording, play the mission once with the same seed so you know which sol the storm arrives on. Use
"Fast-forward" to reach it off camera, then record from the morning the sunlight drops.

Checklist:

- [ ] 30 seconds or less (check the file, not the timeline)
- [ ] Shows one whole sol: report, bet, reveal
- [ ] Shows a number being tapped through to its NASA source
- [ ] Captions readable on a phone
- [ ] No under-18 faces or voices; AI-made assets watermarked
- [ ] Link opens in a private window

## Slide deck instead (at most 7 slides)

The deck must show the team name, the names of the team members and the challenge.

1. **Title.** SolCast, the tagline, team name, members' names, "Challenge: Build a Junior Astronaut Mission
   Trainer".
2. **The problem.** Space games for kids either stay abstract or invent their numbers, so kids never feel a
   real trade-off.
3. **The game.** One screenshot of the Outpost screen: three gauges, four crew, one slider, one emergency
   button.
4. **Real data decides.** The reveal screenshot with the source card open. Curiosity's weather record: sunlight
   and overnight lows for every sol.
5. **The 2018 dust storm.** A strip of the rover's measured sunlight, Very High falling to Low in eight sols and
   staying there, next to the crew huddling.
6. **What kids learn.** Keep a reserve; test a forecast before trusting it; air, food and warmth come out of
   one battery. Plus the "How it works" page: NASA measurement, physics or game rule for every number.
7. **Next.** Classroom tournaments by mission code, a solar storm shelter mission from NASA's particle records,
   an Advanced Lab where older students build their own forecasters.
